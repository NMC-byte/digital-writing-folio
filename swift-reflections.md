### What are some of the constraints and tensions that Hayles notes have motivated writers to experiment in the field of E-Lit?
Hayles’s essay on E-Literature and what it is provides some topics that demonstrate a constraint or tension. For writers practicing E-Lit, it is another medium in which they could gain perspective or a new angle on the current decade, more so the time they live in. “To this I would add it creates practices that help us know more about the implications of our contemporary situation.” (Hayles, 2007) 

Furthermore, it is a medium which allows for those of different expertise or skills to cooperate amongst themselves. It could serve as a stage of tension for one to learn and practice to new or niche areas of what’s often overshadowed by traditional writing. “....we understand electronic literature not only as an artistic practice (though it is that, of course), but also as a site for negotiations between diverse constituencies and different kinds of expertise.” (Hayles, 2007)

Perhaps it could also be preservation/archiving itself as a major point of contention. Hayles describes how, opposite to traditional writing, E-Literature lacks the mechanisms for its own preservation. It is a major tension and fear for one’s work to disappear, to no longer function. While this may dissuade writers from practicing E-Literature, Hayles mentions resources that are available in preserving the works writers create, either through online archives such as the Electronic Literature Collection, or “Acid-Free Bits.” Truly creating something worth preserving or capable of withstanding time can be a huge motivator for many writers.

### What defines the ‘Three Generations’ of E-Lit that Flores refers to?
The three generations Flores refers to are the works of E-Literature created during the periods of the development of electronics and digital infrastructure. Flores regards the first generation of E-lit to be from 1952 to 1995. The infancy of computers and programming, and so the small foundations of E-Literature limited to a few initially. Flores describes the second generation of E-Lit to be the integration of web-based interaction and multimedia. The third generation follows the mass adoption of these systems by the masses through social media platforms and web services.

These three generations describes the growth and stages E-Literature took to become what we regard it as today.

### Do you agree with Beren’s argument that Instagram poetry is “indivisible from the surveillance capitalism infrastructure of social media” Also: What does the term ‘non-trivial effort’ mean when it comes to E-Lit?
I do agree with Beren’s argument. Instagram as a platform benefits itself most when user attention and retention is absolute. Algorithms do cater well to our interests and hooks, including when an instagram user does show interest in a topic such as poetry. 

Non-trivial effort in E-Literature could be a term used to describe a work utilizing its space in it’s entirety. A work of E-Literature has the technical capabilities far exceeding that of traditional works, more than simply scrolling through text on a web page.

## Swift Reflection
Have you been working with this iterative method? If so, note a particular example of how this method has worked for you as you have been developing your idea. If not, note down something you would like to test and iterate as an immediate next step.

During the development of my project, I used an iterative process by experimenting with different structures in Twine and adjusting them based on how they functioned. One example of iteration occurred when I initially attempted to create several different narrative branches at the beginning of the story for multiple endings. While this idea seemed interesting, it quickly became confusing to manage and made the story difficult to navigate. I realized that too many choices early in the narrative, I feel, weakened the clarity of the experience. As a result, I revised the structure so that the story begins with several main emotional pathways, “Fine” and “Sad,” which then lead to smaller variations later in the narrative. This change made the project easier to design and allowed the reader’s choices to feel more meaningful.
